# Business impact and loss quantification

> Scenario-based fraud loss model for the Spark Driver last-mile delivery platform.
> All figures are estimates derived from publicly available industry benchmarks,
> FTC/CFPB enforcement materials, and Incognia 2025 State of Fraud data.
> They are not Walmart proprietary figures. They exist to demonstrate structured
> business-case thinking, not to make claims about actual Walmart losses.

---

## Why this document exists

A fraud detection system is only worth building if the prevented loss justifies
the cost of building and running it. This document quantifies what each of the
six attack families Sentinel detects costs the platform, what Sentinel prevents,
and what the operational ROI looks like at three loss-pool sizes.

A program owner speaks in dollars prevented. This document is that conversation.

---

## Attack family loss model

### 1. GPS spoofing and fake delivery completion

**Mechanism:** Driver uses a mock-location app to simulate trip completion
without physical movement. Earns payout and incentive without delivering.

**Per-incident cost:**
- Average Spark Driver payout per trip: $8–$14 (public Spark earnings disclosures)
- Incentive multiplier if at campaign milestone: 1.5–3×
- Estimated per-incident loss including incentive: $12–$42
- Customer refund triggered by non-delivery: $15–$65 (order value)
- Total per-incident exposure: **$27–$107**

**Scale estimate:**
- Industry benchmark: 1.6% of gig-economy trips show location anomalies
  (Incognia 2025)
- At 1M monthly trips: ~16,000 suspicious trips/month
- Conservative fraud conversion rate of those anomalies: 15%
- Monthly exposure: 2,400 incidents × $67 midpoint = **~$160K/month**

**Sentinel detection:** Signals 01, 02, 23 (impossible travel, GPS teleportation,
impossible transit). Estimated catch rate at current thresholds: 70–80% of
organised GPS spoofing operations (multi-account, tool-assisted). Single-incident
casual spoofing: lower.

**Pre-payout prevention value:** If Sentinel holds payouts for CRITICAL-band
GPS cases before settlement, 70% catch rate × $160K/month = **~$112K/month
prevented**, or **~$1.3M annually** at this trip volume.

### 2. Bot-assisted batch grabbing

**Mechanism:** Automated script monitors offer feed and accepts high-value batches
in sub-second latency, draining supply from legitimate drivers. Direct financial
harm is primarily indirect: legitimate driver churn, increased support costs,
erosion of platform trust.

**Per-incident cost:**
- High-value batch stolen from legitimate driver: $18–$35 opportunity cost
- Support contacts generated by displaced legitimate drivers: $8–$12 per contact
- Estimated cost of one active bot account per week: **$200–$400 in displaced value**

**Scale estimate:**
- Industry benchmark: 3–7% of gig platforms experience organised bot activity
  (Incognia 2025, extrapolated)
- Conservative: 50 active bot accounts at any time
- Weekly exposure: 50 × $300 = **$15K/week = ~$780K/year**

**Sentinel detection:** Signal 24 (bot-assisted batch grabbing, sub-800ms median
accept latency in bursts of ≥5). Detection confidence: HIGH for organised bots;
MEDIUM for casual scripting.

---

### 3. Coordinated fraud rings (multi-account, shared device/payout)

**Mechanism:** Organised operation runs multiple driver accounts from shared
infrastructure (one device, one bank account), coordinating to maximise incentive
harvest and trip volume while evading per-account detection.

**Per-incident cost (per ring):**
- Average ring size detected in Sentinel demo: 2–8 accounts
- Average daily payout per account in active ring: $80–$150
- Duration before detection without Sentinel: 14–45 days (industry median for
  organised fraud)
- Loss per ring without detection: 5 accounts × $100/day × 30 days = **$15,000**

**Scale estimate:**
- Conservative: 10 active rings at any time on a large platform
- Annual exposure without detection: 10 rings × $15K = **$150K**
- With early detection at day 7 instead of day 30: savings per ring = $100K × 23
  days / 30 = **$77K per ring caught early**

**Sentinel detection:** Signals 05, 07, 25, graph layer. The graph layer's 1.40×
ring multiplier reflects the near-conclusive evidence value of shared entity
membership. CASE_001 is the canonical example.

---

### 4. Incentive and promotional abuse

**Mechanism:** Driver games campaign mechanics to reach bonus thresholds by
manipulating trip count, acceptance rate, or completion metrics. May involve
GPS spoofing, fake orders, or coordinated customer collusion.

**Per-incident cost:**
- Incentive payout triggered fraudulently: $25–$150 per campaign
- Cost of promotional campaign designed to drive genuine growth: full campaign
  budget, partially captured by fraudulent actors

**Scale estimate:**
- Industry benchmark: 34% of merchants/platforms report incentive abuse
  (Incognia 2025)
- Conservative: 0.5% of campaign participants gaming the incentive
- At 10,000 campaign participants, $50 average incentive: 50 fraudulent claimants
  × $50 = **$2,500 per campaign run**
- 12 campaigns per year: **$30K/year in misappropriated incentive**

**Sentinel detection:** Signals 08 (incentive cliff), 12 (reward-only behaviour).

---

### 5. Account takeover and payout mule activity

**Mechanism:** Attacker compromises legitimate driver credentials, changes payout
instrument, then rapidly completes trips (real or simulated) to extract earnings.

**Per-incident cost:**
- Average account takeover payout before detection: $200–$800
- Reputational cost to displaced legitimate driver: support burden, earnings loss
- Regulatory exposure: unauthorised access to personal financial account

**Scale estimate:**
- ATO losses across gig and marketplace platforms: $13B globally in 2024,
  forecast $17B in 2025 (industry benchmark)
- Platform share of global ATO varies; even 0.01% of $13B = $1.3M exposure for
  a platform of Spark's scale

**Sentinel detection:** Signals 13 (deactivated re-entry), 19 (bank change before
payout), 17 (device hopping), composite score spike.

---

### 6. Refund and delivery fraud

**Mechanism:** Driver marks order delivered without delivery, or customer colludes
with driver to claim non-delivery on delivered orders. Generates refunds while
driver earns payout.

**Per-incident cost:**
- Customer refund: $15–$80 (order value)
- Driver payout retained: $8–$14
- Total per-incident: **$23–$94**

**Scale estimate:**
- Abusive return/refund claims cost US retailers $103B in 2024 (NRF data)
- Delivery platform share is smaller but significant; 0.3% claim rate on 1M
  monthly deliveries = 3,000 claims × $58 midpoint = **$174K/month**
- Fraud conversion rate of claims: estimated 8–12%
- Monthly fraud exposure: **$14–$21K/month = $170–$250K/year**

**Sentinel detection:** Signals 11 (claim concentration), 20 (driver-customer
collusion pair), 03 (geofence miss), 04 (zero-dwell pickup).

## Sentinel ROI at three loss-pool sizes

| Annual preventable loss | Sentinel catch rate | Prevented loss | Platform cost (est.) | Net benefit |
|------------------------|-------------------|---------------|---------------------|-------------|
| $1.5M (conservative) | 65% | $975K | $400–600K | $375–575K |
| $3.1M (mid) | 70% | $2.17M | $500–750K | $1.4–1.7M |
| $5.7M (high) | 75% | $4.28M | $600–900K | $3.4–3.7M |

**Planning implication:** If the annual preventable loss pool exceeds $2M, Sentinel
pays back in year one. If it is below $1M, a lighter rules-only approach with
fewer ML layers may have a better cost-benefit profile. The right answer depends
on actual operational loss data, which this portfolio implementation does not have
access to.

---

## What changes the ROI most

Three levers, in order of impact:

**1. Pre-payout vs post-payout catch rate.** Catching fraud before payout
settlement has 3–5× the ROI of catching it afterward because recovery rates
on disbursed funds are low. The HOLD band in Sentinel is specifically designed
to pause payouts for CRITICAL cases pending analyst review.

**2. Ring detection speed.** Each day an organised ring operates undetected costs
approximately the same amount. Catching a 5-account ring on day 7 vs day 30
prevents 23 × $500/day = $11,500. Graph-layer ring detection is therefore
the highest-value component for platforms with organised fraud exposure.

**3. False-positive rate.** Every wrongly held payout costs approximately $25–$50
in support contacts, analyst review time, and driver dissatisfaction. At 2%
false-positive rate on 500 daily flagged accounts, that is $250–$500/day in
operational cost. Signal-level FP documentation in Sentinel is not just good
practice — it is directly cost-relevant.
